I finished this book a couple of weeks ago, but I found time only now to post my thoughts on it.

This book is a memoir of Facebook's ex-policy maker who describes her experience at Facebook and how it turned from a project focused on connecting people into a project blamed for spying on people, selling their data, manipulating elections, and destroying democracy.

From the beginning, the author makes it clear that Facebook is controlled by different heads, not the founder and CEO, *Mark Zuckerberg*. Mark is just a nerd who has social anxiety about talking to people. All his public appearances are well practiced and organized. One recent example is this article: [Mark Zuckerberg is Building a Photorealistic AI Avatar of Himself to ‘Engage’ With Employees](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/14/mark-zuckerberg-is-building-a-photorealistic-ai-avatar-of-himself-to-engage-with-employees/)

In my opinion, this is one of the worst things that can happen to society. The person who built his empire on a social network has social anxiety. C'mon. Moreover, he does not want to be bothered by his employees so that he can think of better—money-making—ideas which he is unable to manage.

This raises a critical issue about social network (Facebook) and its influence on society and democracy. Since the CEO is not interested in running the social network, the newly hired heads of department—which are usually friends or exes of other department heads—run it in their own way. Obviously, these people mostly cared about making more money, no matter if the world is destroyed. One example is the [Myanmar crisis](https://time.com/6217730/myanmar-meta-rohingya-facebook/), for which Facebook was clearly found to be responsible. The author talks about it with details in the book.

The author also shows that behind the pompous show of Facebook's success, there is so much going on behind the scenes. There were many incidents that were hard for the author as a woman, such as sexual harassment by one head, which was dismissed internally and never escalated to Mark. On the contrary, they fired the author. This clearly shows that Facebook's internal policies are very different from what they tell the public and politicians.

Reading this book further decreased my trust in these online companies. Apart from recent whistleblowers leaking internal information, I had an intuition that these big corps might be doing wrong, but this book cleared my doubts.

Highly recommend it. **4 stars**
