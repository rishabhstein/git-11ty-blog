Just finished this book yesterday, and the words I have to describe the book are *tragedy, tragedy, and tragedy*. This book kept me at the edge from beginning to end. I was anxious while reading every single page, fearing that something bad would happen. So for sure, it is not a book for lighthearted people or for those who have some past trauma.

The story is set in Soviet-era *Kabul, Afghanistan* up to  the time when US army came to fight Taliban. There is no single protagonist in this book, but in a web of characters the focus is on two women, *Mariam and Laila*, whose fates are bound together through many tragic changes in life, and *Afghanistan*.  When Soviets left the country, the political ground shifted and Afghani warlords took over, bombing houses, hospitals, schools, and almost everything. Freedom was gone, especially for women, food became scarce, and no one was safe. Everyone hoped for a miracle, and one seemed to rise in the refugee camps of Pakistan, the Taliban.

Sadly, when Taliban took over, the situation became worse. They imposed strong sharia laws where reciting poetry, singing songs and studying science all were sinful. The government even forbade to show a flamingo bird without pajamas because showing its naked leg was a sin. Now imagine the situation of women that time. There was a lot of violence against everyone. The way *Hosseini* described it will make your heart sink.

It's a classic *Hosseini* style, I guess, to write every scene in a tragic way. This made me feel both anxious and scared. I wanted to run away from the book but also to finish the story. I understand that he tried to describe the real situation of Afghanistan to make people aware of real struggle of Afghans in war, and not just how it looked on American televisions. And he succeeded in his goal.

The ending of the book is beautiful and left me in tears and rumination. So for those bold heart people, I recommend it with **4 stars**.
