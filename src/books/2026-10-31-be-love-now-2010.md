I heard about this book and spiritual transformation of its writer, a Harvard  professor and clinical psychologist,*Dr. Richard Alpert*, from hard core scientist to a life long follower of [bhakti yoga](https://notes.rishabhps.com/posts/yoga/) under [Neem Karoli Baba](https://notes.rishabhps.com/posts/neem-karoli-baba/).

Although the original story of transformation is in another book, In this book he recalls old times where his scientific mind struggled in believing in any religion and with the grace of his guru he became a  believer. On top of it, he shares his life experiences from that time on to the time of writing the book during which he observed life changes and health crisis—he got half paralysed body.

In later part of the book, he introduces a few Indian yogis who used to live in India in past centuries. The accounts he mentions are mostly based on third person stories.

Overall the book is beautiful. Even though the life became tough for  *Ram Dass* but he talks about bliss and divine grace, a hard thing to imagine in modern times. He explains many deeper concepts using his own accounts and experiences, hitting the right spot of reader’s doubt. For instance:

>  “I’ll read Ram Dass’s book , but I’m going to sit and judge it . ” Forget it — because the judge has designed the game so that the judge won’t have to change , and says , “Anything that doesn’t fit in with the way I thought it was , I reject . ” We have categories for that — it’s “ weird ” or it’s “ occult ” or it’s “ far out ” or whatever we want to call it . It’s a way of putting it somewhere else so it doesn’t blow our scene up . That’s what the judge’s function is , so the scene doesn’t get blown up .”

Reading someone else’s journey is always inspiring for me, especially when I can see myself struggling the same way they did. I cant recommend this book enough, **5 stars**.
