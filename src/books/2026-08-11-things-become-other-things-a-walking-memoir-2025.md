Book comments: 

Memoir is one of my favorite genre for reading books. A cherry on top is when its about traveling. So basically a traveling log is the most exciting thing for me. After all I don't need to spend too much money being an [armchair traveler]().

*Things Become Other Things* is a walking memoir of (and by) *Craig Mod* who came to Japan in his early twenties after escaping from a depressing life in a small city of United States. He developed a habit of walking as a mental therapy which helped him overcome his past guilt and traumas. 

Throughout the memoir the author honors the memory of his childhood friends who died in teenage years when Craig was far from him. He shares and discusses the sad incidents, awkward moments to cope up his guilt during walks. Occasionally, he describes the scenery and cultural differences to his diseased friend as if he was with him. He often wonders how different the world would be if his friend were still alive.

Another nice thing, which I liked even more, is so called *Book of John*. It's a metaphor, and not a real book, about author's close friend John who lives in Japan and has a deep knowledge of Japanese history. John introduced the author about the whole idea of long walks. So the occasional messages of John during author's walk are the snippet's of *Book of John*.

The author describes it in a chapter *The Book of John*:

>The “Book of John” applauds a well-timed potato chip. It compels a man to fly to *Aomori* in the rain to drive around for five hours to eat apple pie and do some “research.” Could this research have been done on the internet? Absolutely. Would it have been in accord with the “Book of John”? Absolutely not. Because, according to foundational principles animating the “Book of John,” something electric happens between theory and practice, and being there—in the place, breathing the air of that place and stomping across the ground—brings that magic, that  ineffable and wonderful electricity, to both the practitioner—the “researcher”—and those who may eventually get the information. You have to believe this.

It's a beautiful book to read. I highly recommend with **4 stars**.
