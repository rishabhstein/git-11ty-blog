I watched this movie a couple of days ago but didn't have time to post it because of moving out from one place to another.

Anyway, Leonardo De Caprio is very picky in selecting a movie and that's why the expectation of movie to be good and unique is obvious.

This movie hits the backbone of current American political problems: migration and border control. As most of us are aware of American politics right now, the movie shows a group called **French 72** who revolts against the government to defend migration and open borders. However, the government tries to suppress this revolt by arresting and killing the members, which results the group to go undercover.

Leo played a militant of *French 72* who loves another member of the group. They both actively participate in *French 72*. However, after the girl gets pregnant, Leo tries to be a responsible father and starts ignoring the group. The result of this behavior is that his partner leaves him alone with baby.

Life goes on for seventeen years without any trouble. The baby girl grows up. Meanwhile, Leo, while taking care of her, becomes addicted to alcohol. They almost forget their past while living happily. But one day trouble knocks at the door again.

Fast-forward, the last few minutes of the movie are intense but with a fatherly humor. I love that part when Leo looks for his daughter, driving an old car whose engine sounds like a Vespa scooter engine. It felt like those moments when a father is coming to save his kid. Being old, he can drive only so fast but rest assured he is coming.

I highly recommend watching it. It deserves **5/5** stars.
