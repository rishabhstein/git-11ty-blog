---
title: "A journey into fatherhood"
date: 2026-01-27
tags: ["life", "explore"]
---

Since I wrote the last long post, I've had a burst of inspiration for writing about many different ideas, especially involving another important event of my life—last year was already full of these life-changing events. To write a post, I tried to focus on one idea multiple times; however, due to my ADHD mind, it was not easy. After many failed attempts, yesterday I finally convinced myself to share my journey into fatherhood—coming from someone who was once ideologically opposed to having children.

Until five years ago, in my late twenties, I used to argue about human overpopulation on Earth and its consequences. I believed that man-made global warming, over-consumption of resources, massive production of chemicals and waste were degrading the planet and making the lives of other species worse. In several debates, I defended the view that, given the current situation, the next generations might need to go through hell to survive. Thus, not reproducing was a better choice.

A few years later, my niece was born. It was the first time in my life that I held a child in my hands. The experience was deeply touching. I felt as if something moved in my heart, a warm feeling appeared from nowhere, completely unexpected and unintentional. From day one, I have had a special bond with my niece (now five years old) that sometimes makes other family members jealous. My niece showed me an emotional side of my brain that was suppressed by my rational brain a long time ago. Because of her, I became more empathetic, generous, kind, and humane.

Slowly, I learnt that my strong ideological views had no real basis. They were merely superficial ideas derived from rational thinking. The being-human part was missing from them. So I gave them up and opened myself for deeper life experiences, including those boring, normal-looking ones, such as getting married and having kids.

On the tenth day of this year (2026), my son was born, when I was away for my research work. I again had very similar feelings as I felt at the time of my niece's birth. The news of becoming a father brought me so much happiness that the echoes of those moments are still around me, two weeks later. The feelings are hard to express in words, but in simple terms, I just feel happy.  However, I was also nervous earlier that day when my wife went into labour pains. But later I calmed down after learning that she did a remarkable job—she even inspired her friends and other girls in the neighbourhood by showing her strength of bearing the pain—and both mother and baby were healthy.

Now holding my baby in my arms, seeing it smiling and crying, makes me wonder about the workings of the human mind. With evolution, it grew strong in speculation as it helped us in our survival. But the real depths of it are still hidden from us, waiting to be revealed only after experiencing life. Nowadays, I restrain myself to creating theories based on mere speculations and wait for the experience. I try to live life to the fullest without overindulging in anything and without judging or dictating others. I respect and appreciate the time I have.

My earlier arguments are still valid and not entirely wrong. That said, the domination of an entire planet by a single species is a complicated issue. Before us, it was the dinosaurs, and they are long gone. Perhaps we will see the same fate. Only time will tell.
