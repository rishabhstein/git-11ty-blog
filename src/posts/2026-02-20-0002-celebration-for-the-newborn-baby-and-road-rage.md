From yesterday, one recurring thought I have is about Indian driving conditions. A word stuck in my mind is *Road-Rage*. I lived in the EU for almost seven years, and I am used to nice driving conditions there. Although there are differences in driving behavior among EU countries, ignoring some arseholes and particular countries, I feel that EU drivers are still very responsible in general.

Here in Northern India, on the other hand, driving feels like playing a video game. It's a common view to see people overtaking from both sides, even when there are only two lanes and three cars; Need for speed in real life. 

The situation is a bit better on expressways where there are 4-6 lanes for one way, and worse when there is one lane for both ways. But the careless driving is clearly visible everywhere. Ironically, when I drive here in India, I feel I am the most careful driver around, while other drivers see me as the dumbest one, for driving so carefully (under speed limits) and ignoring unnecessary overtakes.

The reason for such careless driving is the ease of getting a license. People can get a license without even giving any driving test. Ofcourse, it's not true in big cities where corruption is slightly less. However, in smaller cities, one can find an agent who has a connection inside the office. This agent can take a bribe, on behalf of some officer, and help you get a driving license. 

I myself got the license ten years ago, without giving any exam (a shame). Fortunately, by travelling around, I learnt how important it is to follow the traffic rules. These rules are not just another government restriction to decrease freedom of life. They are there to save lives. After learning the importance of these rules, I sent my father to a driving school, and he became a sincere and confident driver. I am proud of him :)

Knowing all this chaos, I still try to keep my calm while driving. It's frustrating when someone honks at your ass so many times so that he can wrongly overtake you, but I just ignore them. Capitalism is rising, and so is the number of cars on the street. The Indian government is trying to make the traffic rule strict by increasing the penalty, but I think it will take a generation to see the changes. Sadly, till then, so many lives will be lost.
