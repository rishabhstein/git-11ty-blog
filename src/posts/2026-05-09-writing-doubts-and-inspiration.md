From yesterday I have been feeling a doubtful state of mind related to blogging and writing. I feel as if I have no important words to say, my opinions are limited, and most of the bloggers I follow are just complaining about someone or something in their posts. There are a few exceptions, such as [Appadict](https://appaddict.app/) or [Andrew's linkdumps](https://82mhz.net/posts/2026/05/linkdump-no-106/), which have a certain theme, but for others there is nothing new to write. The most common topics of complaint are **AI** and **social media**.

This week I wrote two posts but never published them because they contain micro thoughts on different topics, or what I am feeling at the moment of writing. There is no coherent structure in them, which might not be good for a reader if I post them. ~~Wait a minute - am I thinking about the reader now?~~

This chain of thought led me to read my earlier posts from last year. I found that I wrote on a variety of topics, from my career struggles to books on philosophy and the supernatural. But now I feel empty and underconfident. My mind does not want to express anything, or if it does the topic seems mundane and unimportant.

To resolve this problem I visited the blog of [Visa](https://visakanv.com/1000/), who, to my knowledge, started posting 1000-word vomits a day back in 2012. I dug through the older posts, mostly the first twenty or thirty, to see how his attitude changed with the everyday struggle of doing the word vomit. What I found is a difference between my challenge and his. He never claimed he was going to do it every day. His plan was to do a thousand entries with a thousand words each. He is still doing it after more than ten years.

While what I did was force myself to write for fifty days in a row, which I now think was too much. Now I can see it.

I was also checking his mental health by analyzing the data from his blog. This method is common in the physics community to study a complex system. What I did was take all of Visa's posts and ask Claude to plot usage of positive words every few months. By positive words I mean happy, healthy, growth, beauty, etc. Interestingly, this would have taken me quite a while if needed to be done by myself, but Claude did it in a few minutes. I saw how his posting frequency changed over time and how, in different phases of life, he started using different words. For instance, in his late twenties to early thirties he started using words like beauty, calmness, and peace.

However, to be honest, I felt a bit guilty for looking into someone else's mind. Sorry, Visa. I might have a similar pattern, and even though it feels uncomfortable that someone can spot a hole in my understanding, I feel OK with it. A contradiction, right?

Reading his entries gave me a boost of motivation. I feel I can also write in a similar way and not think much. The only difference is English being my second language. I do not think I can write as fast as Visa did. When I try, I make so many mistakes that I feel shy.

Anyway, I finished today's target of five hundred words. See you next time.
