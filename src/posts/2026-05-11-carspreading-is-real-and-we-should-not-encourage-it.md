Hello. Good morning. It's Monday, and I am sitting in my office figuring out what I want to do today—experiments, data analysis, or computer modelling. I am more inclined to analyse the data for now.

The reason is laziness, which has something to do with last night. I went to bed late, around 2 a.m., when my regular time is around 10–11 p.m. Since it was Sunday, I took a nap in the afternoon. Then in the evening, when I was not feeling sleepy, I started searching for second-hand cars because I need to buy one. My current one, an old Hyundai i10, is getting older and its registration will expire in three years. So despite it being a nice car, I needed to sell it. If I don't do it, it will go to scrap after the registration expires. It's time to move on.

Last evening I was checking reviews of different Indian cars. Interestingly, what I found is that people tend to choose what is popular even when it's an expensive business. Most people do not want to take risks, so they buy what others have tried. Not much creativity, eh.

I, on the other hand, think differently because I like to personalize things a lot. I usually give more time than usual to decide, but once I decide I accept whatever comes, which often results in a peaceful and memorable experience.

Nowadays, all around the world, [the size of cars is increasing](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cy7vdvl2531o). There is a specific term for it: **carspreading**. Of course not everyone is happy about it because bigger cars need more space. But most customers love it because it's all about how much you get in return for your money. So bigger is better.

In this segment of bigger cars, there is this weird combination of hatchback and SUV known as a crossover SUV. Why not a crossover hatchback? Well, again, bigger (toward SUV) is better. Ironically, these cars look more like hatchbacks than SUVs.

To be honest, I am not against SUVs—I do love them—but I am not sure about this crossover. These fake SUVs look bigger from outside while inside the engine is still from a hatchback.

In the Indian market, there are a few models below 10 lakhs (10k euros), but there are not many choices in this segment. So it's hard to decide which one I like. I usually like unique things, which has something to do with social interaction. Unique things make it hard for people to comment because they do not fall into familiar categories, so people hesitate to comment. Of course the downside is that it draws attention, which I often ignore.

Anyway, the conclusion of the car hunt was none. I still could not decide. There was none in the price segment I want to buy, so I was frustrated. Then, due to looking so long at my Mac/phone screen, I got a headache. I still have it.

I feel the choice paradox is at play. I feel like giving up the car hunt and taking some time to digest the information. In India, Nissan and Renault have a smaller secondhand market, while Tata, Hyundai, and Suzuki are popular, so the latter ones are easy to buy and sell. Feature-wise, Renault and Nissan both are competitive, so their cars look cool, loaded with many nice features. However, the catch is that these features often come in upper models, which increases the price and puts these cars at the border of my budget and therefore less appealing.

Okay, I feel humiliated enough about my English, so I am finishing my target today. See you next time.
