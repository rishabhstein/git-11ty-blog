title: "A lesson learnt about blogging"
date: 2025-11-04

For many days, I have been pondering on a quote from **Marcus Aurelius**.

> If you seek tranquility, do less—or at least only what is essential

The reason to think about it is my inability to post a written blog post frequently, at least once a week or every two weeks. Last month, I mostly posted about the content I consumed, such as movies, photos, and book reviews. The content that was easiest to consume got more posts. So the order of the number of posts was exactly movies, photos, and books, in decreasing value. This also shows that writing a book review—a task I dare myself to do—is harder than writing a movie review.

When I started my blog, almost a year ago, I was fascinated by bloggers who were posting comments and reviews about their online activities, such as books they read, music they listened to, and movies they watched. It was exciting to see that I can build my own world in a similar way. As a result, I started doing everything that others were doing as well as what I liked to do, which was blogging. In addition, I was trying to maintain my notebooks, aka garden, which was a demanding job due to extensive note-taking and managing. 

You can imagine now what went wrong. *It was trying to do too much.*

That’s where the above-mentioned quote comes from. As you may know, I am a big fan of **Stoics** and I try to follow these ancient philosophers. The quote by *Marcus* resonated with me at the moment when I was trying to figure out the answer to my question, which is “Why am I not posting as frequently as my other friends?”.

Based on this discussion (with myself), I understood that blogging is a hobby and I should pursue it carefully so that it does not interfere with my work life. I will still post commentaries about movies, books, and photos, but I cannot promise a full review of everything. I prefer to write more blog posts instead of giving reviews for my content consumption.
