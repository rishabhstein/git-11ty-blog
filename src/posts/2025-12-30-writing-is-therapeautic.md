---
title: "Writing is therapeautic"
date: 2025-12-30:T22:00:00
tags: ["minimalism"]
---

I feel very peaceful whenever I write on paper, especially using a fountain pen. The smooth flow of ink from the pen nib to the paper, while it moves against the roughness of the paper, is so stress-relieving that I can hardly express it in a few words. What I can tell you is how I feel. And I love it. From the very first day, I have been hooked due to its therapeutic effects.

I am writing this post on paper with my favorite fountain pen, and what I am observing is that the fine movements of the pen on my command are so satisfying. It is giving me an illusory sense of life in control (joke!!) of my pen, if not mine. The focus required to control my hand for the precise movements of the pen on paper makes writing so engaging for the mind that the distractions go away, and the mind becomes very focused. This could be the reason that typing is more engaging than writing.

Interestingly, nowadays there are new *E-ink tablets* in the market, such as *reMarkable* or *Onix Boox*. These tablets are marketed as distraction-free and focus-oriented work devices. However, marketing often shows the good side of a product and hides the flaws. Falling into the trap, I bought a  *reMarkable*  two/three years ago.  Most of my experiences were pleasant, as the device delivered what the company promised, but it still can not compete with writing on paper with a fountain pen. The main reason is the human nature of cluttering things, which, after crossing a threshold, brings the distraction back. So very often, I fall back on the pen and paper method, and in those periods, I seldom use the device.

However, readers must be warned that my experience might have an *ADD* bias as I am very easily distractible. So you might be perfectly fine with it. After all, I have seen people using an iPad as a reading device.
