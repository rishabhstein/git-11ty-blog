It's my fourth vomit post. In yesterday's post, I chose a mentally heavy topic, which exhausted all my energy. While writing that post, my internal critic was constantly afraid of not adding enough references. I pushed myself to finish it anyway, but it killed my beginner's enthusiasm.  Now, I am writing this post in the evening, contrary to previous posts, which I wrote in the morning. My today's writing goal is to reach the word target, i.e., five hundred words.

There was also another reason for postponing the writing. For my postdoctoral research, I moved to Brussels a few months ago. Due to immigration formalities, I did not want my pregnant wife to be put under stress. So she stayed with my parents.

After my son was born, I immediately flew to India to join my wife and family. It wasn't easy to take weeks off due to the nature of work; I work with dangerous chemicals in the lab. But my supervisor allowed me to take one month off, and suggested doing one month of work from home. I was excited to have two months with my family, especially to witness such important events. Thanks to my supervisor. :)

I finished my first month last week, and it was a lot of fun. Then I started working half-heartedly. During the week, half of me was involved in celebrations, and the other half was reading articles. Now, tomorrow the second working week will start, which means I should be ready to work with full energy. However, being far from the laboratory, I will work on a new computational project, which involves learning the theory of the problem first. So, I feel a psychological pressure to be productive, but it's not possible till I understand the problem first. This was worrying me throughout the weekend.

--

For the past few days, I was spending a lot of time with my ten-year-old nephew, who came to visit his newborn cousin :). I came to learn from his mother that last year he suffered from migraine attacks due to overuse of screens. However, after the diagnosis, he listened to his parents and did a remarkable job by significantly reducing his phone usage. Now, he spends more time on hobbies: sketching and bike riding.

I was a bit sad to see the real impact of *reels* on a kid in my own family. However, I was also proud of him that he did something which many adults struggle with.

His example reminded me of how dangerous the doom-scrolling culture is for our society. From rural regions to urban regions, from small cities to big cities, and from poor countries to rich countries, social media is ruining our kids everywhere in the world. Now, it is up to us to save them.

I already took certain steps in that direction by creating a *Plex* media server with a kids profile. Now kids can watch their favorite shows anytime from my server. I also [plan to start Plex TV channels, in future.](https://quarters.captaintouch.com/blog/posts/2025-09-20-tv-time-machine-a-raspberry-pi-that-plays-random-90s-tv.html) For offline days, I own both card and board games such as *Uno and Chess*. I try to spend my time with kids around me as much as I can, so that they dont feel bored enough to pick up the phone first. However, only time will tell whether my effort can make any difference.
