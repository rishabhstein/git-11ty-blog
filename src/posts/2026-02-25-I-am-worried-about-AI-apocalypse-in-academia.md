So many topics in my mind, and probably in everyone's mind, are driven by fear. One of the topics is career uncertainties due to artificial intelligence (AI). Although I am aware that there are many other jobs that are under direct threat of AI, I still feel fear for the future of a less vulnerable career, a scientist.

The reason for my fear is less financial and more ideological.

In the previous century, before computers were common, only the most passionate people used to pursue this career. Without computers, they invented, discovered, and contributed so much that now, with so many fancy research tools, we can only dream about it. With time, computers became powerful, and we got the ability to tackle even more challenging problems. However, the computer models weren't new; they were simply built upon the models developed by previous century geniuses using only pen and paper.

Doing science with the help of computers was a lot of fun, and it still contributed to human knowledge significantly. One particular example, which is important for everyone on earth, is weather forecasting. It was—and still is—a hard problem to solve without computers. We need a lot of data points from numerous weather stations. And we need to interpret them using computers to accurately predict local weather.

While the computers resolved many complex problems in our society, it had also created many. For instance, the number of computational studies-oriented articles increased by orders of magnitude. Many of these articles claim results that could not be replicated due to either the unavailability of the code or the poorly written documentation of the code. It is a well-known problem in the scientific community, especially among those who review these articles.

There is another problem in academia related to students[^1]. After the computer became common in teaching, the majority of university students performed worse in theoretical calculations. The more dependent they became on computers, the less enthusiastic they were to do the calculations.

These observations are about average and do not focus on individual ones. Of course, many of these graduates are successful today, in a social sense, as they chose computer-based careers. However, for a scientific career, they were not among the best. Those who were good at theories became good with computer models after proper training. But the vice versa is not true.

Now, after the arrival of AI, I am worried about what will happen to science and its students. From teaching to doing research, everything is getting complicated. The parent company of *ChatGPT*, *OpenAI*, is developing more sophisticated AI models to solve famously hard problems, and ironically failing in most of them. However, the company is achieving its main goal to make it popular among researchers so that they buy the premium expensive models.

I can still hope for a positive impact AI can make on research. But my main fear is the fall of the system, academia. If the researchers incline to using GPTs, who will train the next generation of scientists? This generation will survive, but what about the kids who will come in the next decade? If these kids won't learn, they will rely too much on GPT. In that case, who will question GPT?

Ultimately, in this dystopian world, AI will be treated as a GOD, and if we do not have trained people who will ask good questions.
