I work in academia as a researcher. So, I need to read research articles regularly. It is a demanding task that requires my full attention, as well as critical thinking skills. Once I finish reading a paper, I feel very happy and satisfied that I learnt something. However, it also exhausts all of my energy, making it a high effort and high reward process. This means doing it again requires a lot of push.

Another problem I face in reading articles is due to my ADHD mind, which needs a lot of stimulation. So doing a random interesting task is much more engaging than doing a high-effort job. Whenever I try to read a paper in a PDF reader or in reMarkable2, I feel looking at an alien script written in an alien language. Reading it feels so fruitless that my mind simply refuses to engage with it.

The consequence of this problem is not good. I struggle a lot in reading articles, which, I think, is easier for my colleagues. Even though I am more ambitious about learning, where I want to learn about anything from Archaeology to Physics, I struggle to extract information from interesting scientific articles. 

Of course, reading an article outside of one's expertise is tricky because it takes time to get used to the vocabulary of the field. For instance, if someone wants to read an article in the field of law, first (s)he needs to learn the terms used in the papers. After reaching some level of familiarity, advanced articles can be studied. So I am aware that my expectations are too high. However, my struggle to read articles in my own field is no less.

To tackle this issue, I have experimented a lot. During thesis writing, I spent some cash to buy expensive tools such as an iPad and a reMarkable2. In the beginning, there was a lot of excitement. However, with time, after the excitement phase passed, the problem of attention deficit came back. Now, a year later, whenever I open an article in reMarkable2, my first expression is blank. I feel I have no idea what to do with it. Very often, in this scenario, I end up exploring the webpages of Indie bloggers or wasting time on the internet by random browsing.

Another method I have tried is to read articles in printed format. I found that focusing on the printed version, similar to paper books, is easier than focusing on a smart device. Moreover, I can take notes using my favourite fountain pen and criticise the results directly on the article. It's a very satisfying feeling. 

Although this method is the oldest one, used for a couple of hundred years, it brings a lot of guilt to me whenever I print something. The guilt is related to wasting paper, and therefore, trees. We are already in the middle of a serious climate crisis, and damaging forests is the last thing I want to be blamed for.

The best approach I came up with was by balancing my guilt and shopping pleasure. I found that reading an article on reMarkable2 with a paper and a fountain pen in hand is the most effective way. It worked during my thesis, so it should work again. The only caveat is that it's slow. Reading an article in this way can sometimes take two to three days. But this is my only hope. 

I will test it again and give you an update.
