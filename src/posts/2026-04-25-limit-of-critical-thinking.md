I am ruminating over my writing style, especially after I restarted the word-vomit series. What I feel is that my style of writing a post and word-vomit is very similar. I share my experiences in both types of posts. Moreover, despite calling a post word-vomit, I am usually careful about the texts. So it is never really a word-vomit.

The key difference between my writing of a post and a word-vomit is that a post focuses on one theme, while word-vomit can have two to three chains of thought.

This creates a dilemma in naming the current series, word-vomit series, where I every time ask myself whether today's post is a word-vomit or a regular post. I can also call it *small-thoughts*, similar to what [Ava]([https://](https://blog.avas.space/blog/?q=small%20thoughts)) does.

Anyway, let's jump to today's topic.

--

After so much hard thinking, I decided to talk about writing movie and book reviews and maintaining a digital garden. One of my close friends today asked me why I do not discuss scientific topics or ideas on my blog, especially when my introductory line of the blog—just before the recent design change—was related to the topics of philosophy and physics.

He was right to point this out, as I myself asked the same question many times. The reason, I think, is my limited energy to *think critically.* When I started the blog one and a half years ago, I was planning to do many things. I was excited to try many new ideas in addition to just blogging, such as making my own wiki (a digital garden of my research work) and writing reviews of the books I read or movies I watch.

Among all these ideas, I managed to create and maintain a [digital garden](https://notes.rishabhps.com), where I regularly update old entries and add new ones, which are mostly based on research articles I read. I try to add new theories of pattern formation physics or new patterns I spot somewhere. You can have a look if you are curious about [pattern formation]([https://](https://notes.rishabhps.com/posts/pattern-formation/)).

I also tried to write reviews, but I could not keep up. The review posts became more like my comment posts instead of reviews. The reason was that I overestimated the amount of critical thinking I can do in a single day. I did not consider whether I would have enough energy to write critical reviews of books and movies after a whole day of work.

With time, I realized that it is not feasible to write critical reviews along with blogging and maintaining a digital garden. So now, thinking about writing a blog post on scientific topics adds more mental pressure, especially when my job is doing physics throughout day. 

Furthermore, my job is so intensive that after a whole day of critical thinking, my mind is so messed up that it needs a place to express emotions. So, ironically, this blog turned into a personal blog instead of a scientific blog, where I try to express my struggle with ideas, self-doubts, impostor syndrome, and career struggles.
