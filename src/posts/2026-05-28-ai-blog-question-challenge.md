AI is such a hot topic now a days that rarely any day passes when I do not encounter a single post in my blogroll, which is small, only around twenty people. Majority of the posts are rants, or rants about someone else's rant. Is this a rant as well? Despite that, I still love reading your posts. They give me new insights and make me aware of new issues. So please keep doing it.

Most of the posts can be put into three categories: haters, lovers, and neutral. I am happy to confirm that most of you are  AI haters like me. I even have less to say about AI lovers and neutral people. 

However, one thing is clear: no matter how much we hate AI and the control of big corporations over this technology, it is going to stay. It is a new tool in market and we need to adapt. 

So I thought to create a blog challenge--may be you will find it useful as well. I would love to see your answers, especially [Ava](https://blog.avas.space) and [Manu](https://manuelmoreale.com/). Feel free to post it on your blog.

> 1. How was your first experience with AI models?
> 2. Do you use AI or are you completely against using it?
> 3. Do you have any preference among different models, for example Claude vs ChatGPT? If yes, how do you choose?
> 4. What aspect of AI models do you like and what do you not like?
> 5. How do you feel about AI generated images? Does it annoy you if someone use them in a blog post?
> 6. Internet is flooded with AI slop now, full of generated text, images, audio and videos. How do you filter it from authentic human creation? Do you have a strategy?
> 7. Are you hopeful for a better future with A.I. or a dystopian one?


> 1. How was your first experience with AI models?

I am a skeptical person, and I heard about ChatGPT around 2024 when I started writing my PhD thesis. At that time I was not impressed because I had seen what machine learning models could do before LLMs got popular. I was neither interested in coding agents nor any text writer. I only wanted to improve my writing skills. So, after proudly finishing the first draft of my thesis, I began using GPT to add literature level complex sentences in my thesis. Many people were already ahead in usage by giving it long original texts, but I did not want to give my whole thesis to it as I was afraid of plagiarism. 


> 2. Do you use AI or are you completely against using it?

Nowadays I use it daily but mostly as an alternative to a search engine. Fortunately, for my work I do not need it but when I do, I ask for brief suggestions as if it were an assistant. For coding, I tested GPT once, extravagantly, and it made such a mess that now I prefer to do things myself.
	
> 3. Do you have any preference among different models, for example Claude vs ChatGPT? If yes, how do you choose?

I do have preferences for different use cases. For general purposes its easy to go with GPT or Claude. However, by playing around with different available models in the market, I found that Gemini is good for search, Deepseek is good for strong reasoning, and many unpopular small models that can be run on a PC are good enough to solve my daily tasks. Since I do not want to waste energy, I use GPT or Claude for advance work.

Answering the GPT vs. Claude question, I feel a natural liking toward Claude.

> 4. What aspect of AI models do you like and what do you not like?

Honestly, I have more dislike toward AI models, despite of their ability to solve complex problems that can be solved by humans. Even though I use AI daily, I do not find anything good in it. Yes, it save my time but it also leave me dissatisfied with the work.

What I particularly dislike is that the conversation with it feels like talking to a charlatan. Confronting it still reveals its machine mind, or gotcha, and yet it tells lies with so much confidence.


> 5. How do you feel about AI generated images? Does it annoy you if someone use them in a blog post?

I am fine with their usage in creative ways. Of course, I do not want to read a blog post written by AI, but if you add something AI generated which can convey your emotions I will not mind. But please mention always that it is AI generated so that someone will not feel cheated.

> 6. Internet is flooded with AI slop now, full of generated text, images, audio and videos. How do you filter it from authentic human creation? Do you have a strategy?

Oh! A hard question. I am not smart enough to defeat AI slop. Neither do I have time to deal with it. So I simply walk away. I am not on social media so I do not see much slop. This preserves my critical energy for the content sent by my closed circle.

Blogs can also be a source of AI slop, but I only spend my time on blogs run by genuine people. That said, it takes time to build trust which is easier if you communicate with the author via Fediverse or email.

One trick to recognize AI slop is to spot the struggle of AI models in simulating physics. This works for now and it might improve later.

> 7. Are you hopeful for a better future with A.I. or a dystopian one?

It's clear that this tech is not going anywhere. So I do hope that after norms and regulations are made we will see a shift in its use case. There are more crucial problems of privacy and surveillance by the government itself, but I do hope that it will still be in favor of the state, and not of any corporation.
