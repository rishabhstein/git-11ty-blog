Last two days felt hard, and I have no idea why. From Monday morning (i.e., yesterday) I have felt lethargic. I am still trying to contemplate why this is happening. I feel the culprit is vitamin B12 or alcohol. I am usually B12 deficient due to bad digestion, for which I take supplements. However, I haven't taken any for the past few days, so that might be the cause.

Alcohol, the second suspect, could be a possible culprit. I do not drink on a regular basis but only for socialization (everyone thinks the same). On these occasions I usually have a beer or two. In the past it was more, but I reduced the amount intentionally as it was hurting my life and stomach. However, despite this change, I feel that I have become very sensitive to the effects of alcohol, especially the next day. From my recent experiences I can tell that every time I drink, the next morning is bad; I feel lethargic as if my blood pressure is low. Also, my mind goes blank—the typical ADHD symptoms get worse.

Last Sunday evening I went to catch up with my friends, so I took a beer. The effect was similar to what I have mentioned above. Now, for the last two days I have felt down. It also broke my routine of sleeping early and waking up early. I could not do either. Now, as a consequence, there might be some hidden guilt, which I feel very often nowadays, if I do not respect myself or break my promises [as I mentioned in my previous blog post](/posts/2026-04-17-old-newspaper-like-blog-design/).

It is hard to conclude, but this discussion is clearly indicating that it's mostly alcohol. Last year I stopped drinking and did not drink for almost a year. But since it makes socialization fun, I started consuming alcohol again. However, the body is also changing as I am in my mid-thirties, so the effect is getting worse than I expect.

Now, after coming to this conclusion I feel better. At least my mind sees some clarity. I should use my tricks of self-control and discipline, which I learned from my experiments. I will tell you about those experiments later.

Let's jump to another problem—creating vs consuming. By creating I mean writing, as that's what I like to do in my free time. It's a relaxing hobby if not done with a critical mindset. But it demands time and energy, so if I am tired or in a hurry I won't be able to do it. That's the case nowadays.

Consuming is contrary to creating. When I read, listen to, or watch something I am consuming. For some time it can be fun, but if done too much it can be a problem. Nowadays, most of us consume more than we create. Binge-watching and doom-scrolling are a few examples of our over-consuming habits.

I neither binge-watch nor doom-scroll (social-media-free), yet I feel the dilemma of creating vs consuming. ~~I must have complained about it~~ [I wrote about it in the past](/posts/2026-02-03-content-consumption-productivity-and-lost-art-of-boredom/), as this dilemma is one of the biggest in my life.

My dilemma can be further broken down into reading vs writing. And in reading, the competition is between blog reading vs book reading. Which one should I do?

==I think I can set some rules based on available time. If I have more energy and around an hour, I can write. If I have less energy and around an hour, I can read books. Moreover, if I am short on time, say half an hour, and I am tired, then I should prefer blog posts.==

Oh boy, I should print these rules. It seems a legit solution to my dilemma, at least in theory. I will update the practicality of these rules later.
