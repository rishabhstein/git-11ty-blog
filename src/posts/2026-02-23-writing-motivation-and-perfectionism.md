It's the fifth word-vomit post, and I still feel lost whenever I open the text editor to write the first few words. Thanks to  *Atomic Habits* by James Clear, I know that habit building takes time, around two to three weeks. So I am patient. But the pressure of producing five hundred words after finishing the work day makes it harder to start. Thankfully, [Meadow](https://meados.cafe) and [Visa](https://visakanv.com/1000/) have faced similar problems, so in the moments of confusion, I can count on their experiences.

When I want to get inspiration, I go to check [Meadow's](https://meadow.cafe) word-vomit post with the same number (fifth one this time) as the one I am writing. Since I am closely following his series, after doing the first five posts, I observed a common theme. We both started the word-vomit series in vacations with great ambitions, but when the work days started (fifth entry; yes, this one), we both struggled to fit writing in our schedule. Coincidence or highly probable events!!!

--

Due to overthinking, I felt yesterday that I was a bit chaotic. I finished the word-vomit post—from writing to posting—in around one and a half hours, as I was under pressure to wake up early the next morning and start working afresh. I felt the grammar could be improved, and the sentence could be reconstructed to have better structure. But I also knew that this overthinking can push me into the perfection trap, where I can polish the text as many times as possible, without actually publishing it. So, this experience saved me from the trouble, and I posted yesterday's word-vomit after doing a basic grammar check.

Interestingly, perfectionism appears almost every day in my life, from work to hobbies. And yet I am not trained enough to spot it immediately. Sometimes, it takes a day or two to recognize it. For example, the problem I mentioned in yesterday's word-vomit, related to a new project, turned out to be a perfectionism problem. I started reading research articles a week ago, and most of the time I was trying to understand everything in the thesis. But it can not be done immediately. Every time I tried, my mind jumped into read-everything-mode, getting exhausted in the end. During all this time, I was self-critisizing myself for not being able to progress fast—I am not a beginner in the field—instead of finding the real problem, i.e., perfectionism.

This morning, after wasting a week, I realized what the problem was. So, I decided to focus on the specific topics instead of reading everything. This strategy assumes that I will learn better with focus and a slow exposure to new problems, and not by theoretically reading everything in a short amount of time. By the end of the day, I finished two out of five goals of the project. Although these two are the easiest ones among the five target problems, I was happy to see my progress in a single day.

Here I completed my five-hundred-word goal, and it's 23:40. So I should go to bed.
