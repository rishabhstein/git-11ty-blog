In those days when I have no fixed goal to achieve, my mind wanders a lot. As a result, I end up tinkering with my blog or doing random internet searches. I think it's not bad when there is a weekend, but what bothers me is that I fall into this trap during weekdays.

A distracted mind acts weirdly because it repeats the same thing, which makes it even more distracted. With more repetition, it loses its energy and starts getting frustrated.

Those days when I am focused, I finish my work with calmness and peace. There is a sense of accomplishment after the focus mode. A similar feeling can be observed after a workout session. I feel calm after that as well.

So it's an irony that a mind that seeks calmness acts in contradiction to the very thing (focus) that can give it calmness.

--

My search for a browser that can be used both on Linux and Mac systems and does not prey on my data is still on. Previously I was using Safari on Mac and Chrome on both Mac and Linux. Safari was a good browser for me until I was limited to Apple. For moving from one platform to another, I was using Chrome.

However, both have issues. Safari is a good candidate for those in the Apple ecosystem. Chrome, on the other hand, is universally available and can be used in most of the common OSes, but it is known for privacy issues.

A couple of months ago I heard about [Orion browser](https://orionbrowser.com/) from [Kagi](https://kagi.com). They built an open-source browser for better privacy based on the Safari engine. Initially, I liked it when I was using it on my Mac and iPhone, but the problem of using it on Linux was (and still is) there.

Orion works great on Mac and has features similar to Safari, such as syncing its data via iCloud to my iPhone. However, similar to Safari, it also limits you to the Apple ecosystem. The Kagi team is working on its Linux build and launched a beta version, but it is very buggy. So I need to wait until its stable Linux version is launched.

Yesterday, I saw a post from Loren where they mentioned [Helium browser](https://helium.computer) and how nice it is. So I thought I'd give it a shot.

What I found is that it is an open-source version of Chromium and is strongly focused on privacy. Since its engine is based on Chrome, it is available for all three common systems: Linux, Mac, and Windows. Even though it is in beta, it's way ahead in development compared to Orion. I tested it on both Mac and Linux, and it's working fine on both systems. So I have no complaints except some frustration related to the initial struggle of installing extensions in the Mac version.

Design-wise it is also nice, as it gives me the feeling of using Safari, but it's actually a minimalist version of Chromium. So overall it seems a solid option. However, compared with Orion, I feel that there should be a way in Helium to sync history—maybe there is, I am just not aware of it.

This browser is so privacy-focused that you need a third-party password manager and history manager to sync your data from one system to another. Everything stays local.

I am using [BitWarden](https://bitwarden.com/) and [Hister](https://hister.org) as a password manager and history manager, respectively, but I am unsure whether it can make everyone happy. If you use Google's default services, then you need to install extensions from Google, which makes this browser pointless.

For me, it is a good solution until the Kagi team succeeds in building Orion for Linux.
