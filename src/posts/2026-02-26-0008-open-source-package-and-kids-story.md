Today was a peaceful day. The reason was the finalisation of the scientific package, which I need to use to work on a short research project. The confusion was between two choices: an open-source package, *OpenFoam*, and a commercial one, *COMSOL*. Both are known to be very good at studying fluid physics. However, since I support open-source scientific packages and I have more experience in using OpenFOAM, it was my first choice. But working with this package is not trivial, especially when doing a moderately complex job. So, time constraints forced me to go for COMSOL.

I am ok with the decision, as it has reduced the pressure of the project. Now, I can focus on problem-solving instead of tackling technical issues.

The peaceful time gave me enough time to ruminate about today's topic. So let's get started.

--

On the last Christmas day, I wrote a [short story](/posts/2025-12-25-story-of-krisha-and-green-ghost/) for my five-year-old niece. The topic of the story was, obviously, Christmas. There were several reasons to choose a Christmas story on X-mas Day. One of them was introducing my niece to a different side of Santa Claus, compared to what is sold on capitalistic market.

My niece lives in a small city in northern India, and for her, Christmas is just another holiday of the year, among so many we have. So for her, Santa is an old man with a fancy dress and a weird beard, who brings gifts for kids. You can see the problem of the capitalistic idea here. Parents, without knowing what Christmas really means, have no choice but to buy gifts for kids on this day. I am not against gifts as they are fun :). What pisses me off is that India has a culturally different society, and most of us do not know the significance of Santa Claus. Yet we celebrate it in a capitalistic sense.

Fortunately, living in the EU allowed me to learn more about catholic society. So I thought to forward my knowledge to my niece in the easiest form possible, a short story. In addition to that, I wanted to teach her a small lesson. Remember from school, the typical question teachers used to ask after reading a story, *"What is the moral of the story?"* So that was kind of my goal.

However, nowadays these methods seem to be dying, at least here in India. They are replaced by digital bullshit. For instance, I saw kids in the neighbourhood learning more from YouTube kids then schools. I can not imagine that a YouTube influencer whose qualification is unclear to me is teaching my kids.

Anyway, I  decided to take the matter into my own hands. I wrote the story in a personalised manner, where she is the protagonist. And, I am happy to report that the trick worked nicely. My niece loved it. She heard the story many times. And she still does regularly. Whenever I meet her, she always asks me to tell her the story. So cute.

Interestingly, this story was not only read by her. It turned out that my nephew read the story as well. He told me last week on the [son's celebration day](/posts/2026-02-20-0002-celebration-for-the-newborn-baby-and-road-rage/). Seeing his enthusiasm, I asked him whether he wanted a story about him. In the beginning, he was confused, but later he showed positive signs.

Now, I plan to write another story, involving both my niece and nephew. I hope it will bring some good memories and teach them morals in the most fun way I am aware of.
