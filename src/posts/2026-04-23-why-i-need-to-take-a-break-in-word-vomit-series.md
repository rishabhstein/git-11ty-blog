~~It has been almost a month since I wrote the last—the ninth one—word-vomit post.~~ I posted two word-vomit posts consecutively the last two days and restarted the series. Right now I do not want to promise anything, but the plan is to finish it with heart and no pressure.

I wrote this post almost a month ago as a backup post, so today I am posting it. The idea is to tell you why I could not keep up with the series.

At the point when I stopped at the ninth post, I had started to like the word-vomit series. My momentum was building up, and the writing resistance was decreasing with each post. I felt at that time that my thoughts could flow through my hands to the keyboard smoothly. However, when I was nowhere near finishing the fifty-post target, I lost momentum and stopped writing new posts. I am ashamed about it.

However, there were specific reasons. After publishing the ninth post on Friday, I needed to drive my family to my uncle's place, which was around 200+ kilometers away. The trip needed to be short, limited to the weekend, due to my weekday work-from-home schedule. So, in crazy Indian traffic conditions, I was driving for the next two consecutive days. Even though the trip was nice and we fulfilled my uncle's wish to see my newborn son, the driving exhausted me. There were incidents on the road that kept me on edge the whole time. Too much vigilance drained my mental energy which took almost a week to recover. 

The following week I also could not start despite being aware I had broken my promise. You see, once there is a break, the promise is already broken. However, I am not that cunning with myself, and it's not a good reason to justify the prolonged break. I think here my mind is trying to hide or avoid explaining the emotions. So I will explain.

You might be aware from past word-vomit posts that I came to India for two months to spend time with my newborn son. The week after the above-mentioned trip was the last week of my stay. I was a bit sad that I needed to leave my son and wife in India due to visa formalities and stay far from them for a while—it could be six months. Those were hard emotions to process, so that whole week I was just spending time with my family.

Then came my travel when I needed to fly from India to Europe. Due to the Iran war, a lot of flights were canceled. I was nervous again. However, it was pure luck that I changed my itinerary from Middle Eastern airlines to Finnair a week before I started my vacation. Phewww!!! I came back as planned, and from the next morning, work started at full intensity. It took me almost two weeks to adapt to this new environment, with cleaning, doing laundry, shopping, and cooking all by myself, for myself.

Now I am gathering all my energy to restart this series. The plan is to finish the target of fifty posts but without any pressure.
