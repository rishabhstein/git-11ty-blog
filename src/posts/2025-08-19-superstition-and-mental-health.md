title: "Superstitions and mental health"
date: 2025-08-19

A few years ago, before I joined a PhD program, I was completely unaware of most of the mental issues such as anxiety and panic attacks. There were inevitable stressful moments throughout my student life, but their effect was minimal. I either brushed them off or fought with them. My beloved family gave me strength, kept me healthy, and loaded me with tons of love. However, in my career journey, my mind changed a lot. In the beginning, it was mostly stress which I knew how to deal with, but with time, as I started writing my thesis, the fear of failure also appeared. As a result, I became nervous, anxious, and also started having panic attacks.

Now you might be thinking why I am discussing it here again as I have talked about my PhD struggle earlier many times. The reason is the relapse of these symptoms. Right now I am on a short break from work and trying to recover from mental issues. I felt I was doing great but last week two big tragedies happened in my far family in my parents’ neighbourhood. I can't go into much detail of these incidents but because of them panic attacks, palpitations, anxiety, all are back. My mind is on constant alert mode, assuming anything can go wrong any time. I am worried about my parents as well as my wife.

Another effect of my constant alert mode is falling into superstition. This is mainly because of common beliefs among locals. When it comes to death matters, India is more mysterious than most of the world. The story of reincarnation, of spirits, and of haunting is so obvious to people that it gives the impression of being a blindly religious country. Even though I studied science my whole life and I do not believe in supernatural, when something happens inside the family, the mind becomes vulnerable. The feeling of eeriness often surfaces when people talk about their superstitions as if they were obviously true.

I want to call these beliefs a complete bullshit, but I am also aware of studies such as **Twenty Cases Suggestive of Reincarnation** by *Ian Stevenson* and **Many Lives, Many Masters** by Brian Weiss. Both of these books defend the existense of supernatural such as spirit and reincarnation. Although I am a bit sceptical of the latter, the author of the former is a well-known researcher of these topics, who documented around 3000 cases of reincarnation around the world. Interestingly, two cases in this book are from my hometown, which were documented around fifty years ago. So I cannot call everything a bullshit.

These are controversial topics where believers and non-believers see each other as morons. But it’s very different when you are in the situation. I think *it’s better to go to a psychotherapist* to treat mental issues than arguing about superstition, which, I believe, will stay among us till the dusk of society.
