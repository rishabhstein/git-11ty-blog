I myself am an immigrant who lived in three different countries, and travelled in tens of others for work, leisure, and exploration. My parent country is India. I grew up in a northern state that is bigger than many EU countries. None of my family members from the past nine generations moved farther than a hundred kilometers. Similarly, I also had no intention of moving far from my family. But the passion for scientific research led me into a scientific career, resulting in me moving around thousands of kilometers inside the country, as well as thousands of kilometers away from it.

During my limited migration experiences, one thing I noticed—and developed a gradual dislike—is that many first-time immigrants develop a strong critical opinion about their parent countries. There are exceptions where the parent country is not democratic, such as Belarus, China, or Iran. However, in other cases, there are not many strong reasons to criticise, except for justifying the decision to leave the home country.

Since my experience is still limited, I will keep my opinion restricted to my own country's people, especially to those who start a YouTube channel to criticize publicly. The target viewers of these channels are, of course, from India. These so-called influencers now live in a completely different environment and tell people back at home what is wrong with it.

I partially agree with them that making people aware of real problems when the government controls the information is a good thing. However, very often, these attempts just add to the existing information chaos in the country, making people more dissatisfied and anxious. And what can they really do? Not much.

I believe that the majority of problems these influencers highlight are often related to their limited migration experience. Some examples are reckless driving, cleanliness, irresponsible social behaviour, and tourist scammers. I found them almost everywhere, sometimes easily spottable, other times hidden. Another common complaint is the rise of right-wing government, which again seems to be a common phenomenon in many countries. Thanks to **Meta**.

These problems always existed somewhere on Earth at a certain time in history. Some people choose to act against them because they believe they can make a change, and it is for the good. I respect them. But those who sit outside the problem and rant about it are not doing anything at all, except monetising the situation.

Now, you may ask what we can do if not act against the problems. I believe the answer lies in a famous quote: **Ignorance is bliss**. Ignore this turmoil and give time to read history and philosophy. They will remind you that whatever is happening around is not new. It has always existed. The only salvation is not far in future, but here around you, in the present.
