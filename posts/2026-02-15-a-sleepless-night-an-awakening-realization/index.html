A sleepless night, An awakening realization

Feb 15, 2026

Tags: #post #life #philosophy #repost

It's 6:50 AM in the morning, and I woke up almost an hour ago after my five-week-old son woke up. My wife was trying to calm him down by feeding him—yes, that is among the few things, including changing diapers, which can calm him down. She succeded to pursuade him, and later both of them fell asleep. I, on the other hand, was stuck with anxiety as my mind slowly drifted toward the future, and I failed to sleep again. Even though I knew that it is the mind's way to ignore the present and to live in the future or past, it was not easy to apply this knowledge. I think it is the case with most of the people who absorb a lot of theoretical knowledge.

Unable to sleep, I decided to check my feed reader. There I saw a post by Meadow from his Vomit series in which he forces himself to write everyday till he reaches fifty posts mark. In this post, he talks about how writing every day changed his way of thinking; he feels calm and has structured thoughts. This reminds me of a popular habit among Stoics, daily journaling, which helped them as well in the Roman era. The limited literature historians have today from Roman republic/empire is the result of that habit.

I wanted to try this journaling habit for the past few months, especially after reading Senecca's letters, and other Stoic books. However, my skepticism always gets in the way. I am still unsure whether it will be effective. And how will I find time?

My past experiences and Meadow's current experiments show that for any good habit, the results take time to appear, no matter how hard you push through. The only way to succeed is to keep on trying and be consistent. Initial doubts, despite being normal and energy savers, will come and hinder the progress. They will try to convince you not to waste energy on such hard tasks, especially if they need consistency. That's why building and maintaining any good habit is hard.

I tried a couple of years ago to develop a routine for running. I pushed myself to live a hard life by sleeping on the floor, waking up at 5 AM, and going for a run regularly, regardless of good or bad weather. With time, the miracle happened as I transformed myself from a couch potato to a 5K runner. It was one of the personal successes that I am still proud of. It boosted my confidence and made me strong mentally and physically. Recalling them still brings joy. However, this habit did not last long. In the following winter, I needed to start writing my PhD thesis alongside working on research projects. So I was forced to take a break, which pushed me backwards and brought me back to zero.

Even though my running routine was disrupted, I still have the happy memories about it, which motivate me to try it again. I am already in the process of building it again—last week I reached the 4K mark—and I am happy about it. However, I never tried daily journaling, so I do not know how rewarding this habit is. I can only trust the experiences of others.

So, conclusively, I am convinced that daily journaling will add something new to my life. I might gain some deeper understanding of my mind or develop a structured thinking process. And, in the end, if it doesn't work, I can always stop it.
