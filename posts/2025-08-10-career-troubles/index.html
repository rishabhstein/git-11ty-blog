Anxiety and Voluntary silence

Aug 10, 2025

Tags: #post #life #career #phd

For the past two days, I have been feeling anxious. I even had a panic attack last night. The main reason is the uncertainty in the offered postdoc position, for which I was selected last April. But due to a new law in Belgium, I can not apply for a visa till I receive a work permit, or a single permit in official terms.

Previously, this permit was obligatory only for company employees who have a regular salary in one country or another. However, for postdocs who have contractual jobs, this permit is senseless. For example, my position is only for two years, and to obtain this permit, it will take at least four to six months--one-fourth of the whole contract duration. Moreover, this duration of many months is between obtaining a PhD and joining the position. It means that I can not start working on the project without the permit, and I need to waste time after obtaining my PhD.

I must say, what an efficient use of my training and skills, especially when the EU paid for it with public money.

And what if they refuse to give me a permit? If this happens, it will add unproductive time in my scientific career, leading to, most likely, a failure in academics.

I feel very sad about it because I never expected that I would fail in academia in this particular way, due to bureaucracy. There are a lot of people who quit academia for a variety of reasons, but I have never heard that someone was forced to do it due to fucking politics.

At this point in my life, I feel strong enough to pursue a career in science, and I do not see any reason to quit. However, only time will tell how long I can survive on this path. I am fighting myself to have patience but the thought of failure is giving me anxiety and panic attacks.

I want to write a lot about it, to express as much as I can. But I also feel a limit in words. Just writing this short text made me feel burned out. The extent of it is such that I decided not to speak. From the morning, I am using sign language to communicate with my parents, pretending as if I were a mute.

Although it might sound extreme, it's a regular practice for me whenever I am overwhelmed. Throughout my PhD, I practiced this voluntary silence because it helped me to calm down in stressful moments. Not speaking removes the burden of being a failure, as I do not need to explain anything to anyone.
