I built my own music collection and why you should too

Jun 4, 2026

Tags: #post #self-hosting

Till my late twenties (now I am in my early thirties) I used to listen to music regularly. During my teen years it was on a bulky cassette player or a Walkman, or on the radio. In later years I switched to portable music players and then to mobile/smartphones. I still remember those moments vividly when I used to get excited about a new song and then go to a nearby music shop to get a copy of it. Each time I needed to ask my mother for the money, and request her not to tell my father ;).

As I get older, I started listening to music less and less. I do not know whether it was really a growing-up thing or I just got overwhelmed by technology, especially the arrival of online services. I enjoyed the music till I had keypad phones but later there were too many things in on my (smart)phones.

It is hard to find the culprit. And, it's not that I have not used iTunes, Spotify and YouTube subscriptions. I have tried them many times but their auto-suggestion feature was not always good. Often times, after finishing my selected playlist, they played shitty songs and killed the whole vibe. The energy required to skip multiple songs and pick a suitable one was too much. Each time I got overwhelmed and canceled my subscription within a month or two.

Overall my music enthusiasm decreased with time, and I accepted it. However, last year after reading many blog posts, especially by Cory, I got some motivation for building my own music library, and getting rid of online music services, completely.

Slowly, I started collecting my favorite songs on my home server so that I can listen to them anywhere. I also started listening to the radio more to find new songs. I will spare you from the technical details except that I use Plex for self-hosting the media library.

The easy accessibility and minimal distraction gave a huge boost to my music listening. I found myself listening to my music collection regularly. What surprised me the most is that it became a matter of self-discovery. Each time I found a favorite song, I felt as if I found a piece of me; a Lego block of my emotional being. I never expected that this small experiment would bring such a big change in my life.

Now after a year of this experiment, I can say that keeping precious things close to myself makes me happier, more confident and relaxed. I dance in the shower, vibe with beats at work, and hum around when I am bored, just because I am not lost in never-ending list of available music in the world.

I encourage everyone to try it.
