Content consumption, Productivity, and lost art of Boredom

Feb 3, 2026

Tags: #post #repost #life

For the past few days, I have been thinking about my strategy for consuming online content, mostly coming from Indieweb. Consuming content here refers to either reading blogs and articles or watching YouTube videos and movies. Since overdoing is an inherent part of my nature, I get overwhelmed very often, no matter what strategy I use. To make matters worse, I combine this leisure activity (content consumption) with learning. Now, someone might argue that this method can enhance productivity, but for me, it has clear drawbacks. For example, I keep a FreshRSS instance for blog reading and a Minifux instance for reading physics today or arxiv articles.

While reading blog articles is relaxing, opening a scientific article is not. It is a demanding task, which I do regularly as a part of my job. Reading scientific text for relaxation leads to ignoring the technical details, which triggers a hidden guilt of not learning anything properly from the article.

There is another disadvantage of mixing leisure with learning. Based on the type of content, my mind operates in different modes: quick skimming of blog posts and focused reading of scientific texts. If I switch between the content types too frequently, it can confuse my mind. Unknowingly, my mind can slip away from one mode to another.

The frustration I face regularly, due to the overlap of different mental modes, is real. Sometimes, I sit in front of the screen and try to read a scientific article with disinterest and fragmented focus, as if I am reading a blogpost. In this situation, I found myself skimming through the scientific text. The mind constantly struggles with the fear of not doing anything properly and drains all of its energy in frustration.

The attitude of overdoing also influences my writing. Due to my training in scientific writing, I am overly cautious about making mistakes. Since English is not my first language, there is always a chance of making logical or grammatical errors. To avoid these errors, I use Grammarly and LLM for proofreading. However, as my trust in these tools is limited, I still try to re-read and correct any posts after algorithmic proofreading. This pushes me into the unforgivable trap of perfection.

The perfection trap has real consequences. The publication process becomes slow and resistant—even the posts as small as one paragraph feels demading. If I extend my writing further in reviewing movies and books, in addition to maintaining a garden, blogging becomes an intensive hobby with a lot of cognitive load.

Many people blame influencers for this productivity cult, where resting or not doing anything is considered a waste of time. According to the influencers, if you just finished your daily job, you should not rest, but you should invest your time in something productive. You can read 1000 books, or write a best-selling novel—anything is possible here. By pushing yourself, you can achieve the heights of success similar to your favorite influencer. However, if you decide to catch a breath, you are just another loser who is sitting at home and not doing anything.

This blame seems true to some extent, as most of my friends have expressed similar feelings of not being productive. However, in my experience, this is not the case. Due to ADHD, my mind naturally crave for more stimulation than an average person. So, despite not using centralized social media, I still have the tendency to be always occupied and never sit still.

Pondering on this matter, a few things become clear to me that seeking productivity all the time—regardless of the source of motivation—has negative consequences, and boredom is crucial for the mind. An always occupied mind is rarely bored, and when the time to do a hard task comes, it gives up. I can see it in my daily life, as during the weekdays I feel lethargic, and on weekend I exhaust myself by writing posts/reviews, playing with the design of this blog, and maintaining my server.

Interestingly, on the topic of boredom, Ava wrote in this month's Bear blog carnival.

It's gotten hard to attain true boredom, or the space to have guilt-free boredom. There is always something I should be doing - either it's work, or studying, or volunteering, or blogging, or household stuff, time with my wife, taking care of friendships, maintaining a server, drawing, journaling, reading books, finally starting the sewing project I keep putting off, and so on.

It aligns very well with my observations. She further referred to her earlier post where she quoted Kate Lindsay:

Boredom is when you do the dishes, run the errand you've been putting off, respond to the text you've left on read. Boredom is when you bring a book to read on the subway or make small talk with the person in front of you in line about how slow the pharmacy is. Boredom is when you do the things that make you feel like you have life under control. Not being bored is why you always feel busy, why you keep "not having time" to take a package to the post office or work on your novel. You do have time -- you just spend it on your phone. By refusing to ever let your brain rest, you are choosing to watch other people's lives through a screen at the expense of your own.

These quotes further support my self-criticism of the content-consuming strategy. As a result, I decided to delete Miniflux instance from the server and NetNewsWire from my phone. From now on, I will use only FreshRss via a web browser to read blog posts. For scientific articles and books, I will use dedicated devices (Kindle and reMarkable) and software (Zotero). I will also try to fix a block of time to read and check FreshRss. I will keep the use of the phone to a minimum, where I will only check FreshRss when I am outside of home, far from a computer. The end goal is to spend time on things I want to be proud of and not on things that pass my time.
